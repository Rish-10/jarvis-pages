Jarvis privacy policy

Last updated 8 November 2026 (read, organise, draft and edit)

Jarvis is a private assistant used only by its owner, Rishi Patel. It isn't available to the public.

What it accesses

With the owner's permission, Jarvis can read, organise, draft and edit in his own Google account. It never sends email and never permanently deletes. Each use is off until the owner switches it on, and only his own requests make changes.

  • Gmail: read mail; organise it (archive, move, label, mark as read); create drafts, which only the owner sends. Moving mail to the Bin needs the owner's passkey.
  • Google Calendar: read events; add, change and move the owner's own events without notifying anyone. Moving an event to the Bin needs his passkey; events with guests are changed only with his approval each time.
  • Google Drive: read, create and edit his files. Moving files to the Bin needs his passkey; sharing is changed only with his approval each time.

Google's permissions for Gmail and Drive would technically allow sending mail and deleting files. Jarvis's own code blocks both: every change it makes must be on a fixed list, and anything else is refused and reported to the owner.

How the data is used

Only to show the owner his own deadlines, events, emails and files, and to remind him of them. The data isn't sold or shared, isn't used for advertising, and isn't used to train AI models.

Storage

On the owner's private server. Sign-in tokens are encrypted. Nothing is shared with third parties. Short extracts may be processed by an AI model provider, only to answer the owner's own questions, under zero-data-retention terms where available.

Limited Use

Jarvis's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

The owner can disconnect at any time in Jarvis, or at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Stored copies are then archived and no longer updated.
